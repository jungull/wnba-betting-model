## 3. Where the player program actually stands

`cbs_v14_player_oof/1` is **not** a player model in the sense the objective requires. It is a
correctly-plumbed *baseline harness* that emits, per obligation:

| target | estimator |
|---|---|
| `p_active` | ridge logistic on 12 Stage-A features, λ chronologically selected |
| `e_minutes_given_active` | walk-forward EWMA over active rows, α selected per fold |
| `attempts_usage` | conditional centre, minutes-α held fixed |
| `player_scoring_distribution` | conditional centre, minutes-α held fixed |

That is **rungs 1 and 2** of the modelling hierarchy in the brief (shifted EWMA baselines,
regularised pooled models). There are **no player random effects, no archetype pooling, no
hierarchical shrinkage, no mixture of experts, and no player-specific models.** Stage C is
represented only by `attempts_usage`; Stage D and Stage E do not exist. Individualisation today
comes entirely from *player-specific recent state* — the EWMA — and not from any pooled structure
that learns league-wide or role-level relationships.

The `player_model_bakeoff_v1` registration (registry row 65) already preregisters the right next
step: a four-arm bake-off with **dynamic hierarchical player profiles** as the highest-priority
arm, under a binding identical-treatment clause. It is blocked on "a shared as-of feature matrix,
manifest-first". `cbs_real_frames/3` **is** that matrix. That is the single highest-leverage
unblock available.

## 4. The minutes bridge does not exist, and must be built first

The registered incumbent is `minutes_ewma_alpha030_v1`: pooled MAE **4.6428** over **13,501**
played rows in 2024–2026, α frozen at 0.30, gates 1/2/3/5 all pass. The micro-gain is
`minutes_twostage_availability_v1`: **4.6057**, improvement 0.0370, CI [0.0116, 0.0613] excluding
harm but **failing** the 0.10 improvement bar — plus a genuinely strong Stage-A result (Brier
0.0796 vs 0.1084 for the expanding prior, bar met). Both figures are **quoted from the registry,
not recomputed here.**

`cbs_v14_player_oof/1` cannot reproduce either, for three independent reasons:

1. **Row universe.** Of 13,501 incumbent rows, 13,450 exist in contract v4 and **51 do not**.
   Conversely 1,058 contract-appeared rows are outside the incumbent universe.
2. **Smoothing policy.** The incumbent freezes α = 0.30 per `MINUTES_MODEL_SPEC`. The arm
   *selects* α per fold — it chose **0.20** for 2022.
3. **Fold structure.** The incumbent tunes on 2021–2023 and tests on 2024–2026 pooled. The arm
   refits every season on an expanding prior-seasons window.

### P-D2 — 51 played player-games are not obligations

All 43 of their games are in the contract; the `(game_id, player_id)` pairs are not. The players
**demonstrably logged minutes** — Odyssey Sims, Marina Mabrey, Moriah Jefferson, Celeste Taylor,
Queen Egbo among them — and the pattern is mid-season signings, hardship contracts and trades:
players with no roster evidence inside the candidacy window who nonetheless played immediately.

This is a **contract universe question, not a modelling question**, and the contract is shared
with the team thread. Per the parallel-program boundary the player program **stops and documents**
rather than changing it — see §6.

The bridge to build: restrict the arm's emission to the shared key set, pin α to 0.30, and compare
on the 13,450 common obligations, **reporting the 51 rather than dropping them.**

## 5. Order of work

Availability and minutes are validated before any broad player-stat work, because every
player-rate forecast is multiplied by minutes.

1. **Unblock generation.** Resolve P-D1. Until the producer gate can actually measure a tree, no
   player artifact can be honestly labelled reproducible, and generating one would manufacture a
   false receipt.
2. **Generate `cbs_v14_player_oof`** across 2021–2026 and validate obligation completeness,
   provenance and exclusion receipts on the artifacts as read back.
3. **Build the minutes bridge** (§4) so the incumbent and the contract path become comparable.
   Preserve the EWMA incumbent and the two-stage micro-gain until they are re-evaluated on the
   corrected contract.
4. **Then, and only then, request scoring authorisation** — see §7.
5. Stage A/B evaluation on the full reporting grid (pooled micro, macro-by-player,
   minutes-weighted, by role, by history bucket, by team, by season, calibration, interval
   coverage, obligation completeness, cost).
6. Hierarchical arm (`player_model_bakeoff_v1`), then Stage C/D/E, then the downstream
   aggregation gate, then RAPM.

Nothing below step 5 is startable without scoring.

### One design constraint that is already settled by evidence

The V4 bottom-up thesis has been tested once, as `bottomup_3pt_channel_v1`, and it **improved its
own channel while degrading the joint forecast** — margin MAE 10.3569 vs 10.1753 over 627 games.
The decomposition shows why: it reduced each side's error variance but **decorrelated the two
sides** (corr 0.3036 vs 0.3258), and since `var(margin err) = var(e_h) + var(e_a) − 2·cov`, the
lost covariance cost more than the variance saved. The team model's two-sided errors share a
common league/pace/era component that cancels in the margin; per-player idiosyncratic noise does
not.

Consequences, binding on this program from the outset: per-side improvement is not evidence;
`corr(e_home, e_away)` is reported for every aggregation candidate; and preserving a shared
component is a **design goal** — which is a concrete argument for hierarchical structure (rung 3)
over per-player models (rung 7) that does not rest on any per-player accuracy claim. Full entry
and its caveat: [`PLAYER_RESEARCH_LEDGER.md`](PLAYER_RESEARCH_LEDGER.md) PE-1.

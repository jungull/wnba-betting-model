## 3. The translation problem, and the distributional assumption (items 2 and 5 of the mandate)

A player-points model emits a **point prediction**. A prop market quotes a **threshold** (a line
plus two prices). Converting the first into a probability comparable to the second requires an
explicit assumption about how the model's own errors are distributed around its point prediction.
That assumption is fit from data here, not asserted, and its sensitivity is measured, not ignored.

**Fitting population (the "out-of-fold error" sample), held out from calibration.** A_primary tier,
fitted folds only (seasons 2022-2026; 2021 is excluded -- it is the degenerate, no-train,
fallback-only fold, `model_was_fitted=false` per `VERIFICATION_REPORT.md`). Critically, this
fitting pool **excludes every row that also appears in the matched market universe** (16,162 of the
21,970 A_primary/2022-2026 rows; the other 5,808 are the ones matched to a props quote). This means
the calibration test in section 4 below is genuinely out-of-sample with respect to the fitted
distribution -- no row used to fit the residual shape is ever scored for calibration.

**Residual diagnostics** (`e = pred_point - actual`, n = 16,162):

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| mean (bias) | -0.0152 |
| std (ddof=1) | 5.159 |
| median | 0.557 |
| skewness (Fisher-Pearson) | **-0.615** |
| excess kurtosis | **+1.296** |
| 5th / 95th pctile | -9.42 / 8.07 |

Answering the mandate's three explicit questions directly:

* **Skewed?** Yes, moderately (skewness -0.61): the model's errors have a longer left tail than a
  normal distribution would produce -- large *under*-predictions (actual >> pred) are somewhat more
  common than large over-predictions of the same size.
* **Heavier-tailed?** Yes (excess kurtosis +1.30, meaningfully above the 0 a normal implies).
  A Student-t fit confirms this quantitatively: log-likelihood profile search over a degrees-of-
  freedom grid selects **df=8** as the best fit, and Student-t(df=8) beats the normal on AIC
  (98,417.8 vs 98,906.1 -- lower is better). **Student-t is the primary translation family**, chosen
  by this criterion, not by eye.
* **Does dispersion scale with the prediction?** Yes, materially. Regressing `|residual|` on
  `pred_point` (OLS, n=16,162) gives slope +0.218 points of mean-absolute-error per point of
  predicted scoring, t=41.9, p<10^-16; the bin-level correlation between mean predicted points and
  residual std across pred_point deciles is 0.963. Players projected for more points have
  proportionally noisier point totals. This is reported as a fitted **sensitivity probe**
  (`het_normal` variant below) -- it is not adopted as the primary translation, because doing so
  after observing it would blur the honest, preregistered-in-spirit comparison between assumption
  choices with post hoc tuning toward better calibration.

**Sensitivity to the distributional choice.** Four named, deterministic variants are computed on
every row and none is silently blended:

| variant | description |
|---|---|
| `normal` | Homoscedastic Normal(bias, std) |
| `student_t` | Homoscedastic Student-t(df=8), scale matched to sample variance -- **primary, AIC-selected** |
| `empirical` | Fully nonparametric: the empirical CDF of the 16,162 fit-pool residuals, no shape assumption at all |
| `het_normal` | Normal with dispersion linear in `pred_point` (the fitted heteroscedasticity above) -- reported as a sensitivity probe only |

On the matched, evaluable universe, the four variants mostly agree: `normal` vs `student_t` RMSE of
0.011 in probability space, 0% of over/under calls flip. `normal` vs `empirical` disagree more
(RMSE 0.043, **16.2% of calls flip**) -- the nonparametric tail shape matters at the margin.
`normal` vs `het_normal` RMSE 0.030, 0% call flips (the heteroscedastic sigma shifts confidence, not
direction, on this universe). **The translation is not free of its assumption**: which distribution
is chosen changes one in six over/under calls when moving to the fully nonparametric alternative.

**Parameter uncertainty, propagated (not discarded).** Game-date-cluster-bootstrapped 95% CI
(seed 20260806, 1000 draws, refit per draw) on the Normal fit's parameters: bias in
`[-0.106, +0.084]`, scale in `[5.069, 5.252]`. The location parameter's CI straddles zero -- the
fit-pool bias is not distinguishable from zero at this sample size, consistent with the near-zero
per-season biases below.

**Season-level stability** (diagnostic, full A_primary population per season, not the fit pool):
bias and std are stable across every genuinely fitted fold --

| season | n | bias | std | fitted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 3,407 | -0.533 | 7.235 | No (degenerate fallback fold) |
| 2022 | 3,939 | -0.013 | 5.408 | Yes |
| 2023 | 4,394 | -0.168 | 5.606 | Yes |
| 2024 | 4,371 | -0.019 | 5.577 | Yes |
| 2025 | 5,213 | -0.042 | 5.611 | Yes |
| 2026 | 4,053 | -0.116 | 5.738 | Yes |

2021's much larger std (7.235 vs ~5.4-5.7 for fitted folds) is exactly what an unfitted,
fallback-only fold predicts, and is the reason it is excluded from the fitting population.

## 4. The translation function (item 3 of the mandate)

**Formula, explicit and versioned (`m13_translation_v1`):**

```
P(points > line) = CDF_e(pred_point - line)
```

where `e = pred_point - actual` is the fitted out-of-fold residual distribution. Every translated
probability is on **matched player-games only** (the 5,966/5,889 universe of section 1), carries the
T1 vendor-asserted timing caveat verbatim (section 2), and makes no timing, CLV, or reaction-time
inference of any kind.

**Every translated row records its provenance**, written to `translation_rows.parquet`
(5,889 rows, sha256 in `FINDINGS.json`): `model_version` (`cbs_v15_player_oof_v5/1`, arm
`cbs_v15_player_oof_v5`, revision 8), the row's own `forecast_cutoff`, `translation_schema_version`,
`pred_point`, `consensus_line`, all four `p_over_*` variants, the market's de-vigged `p_over_devig`,
and their difference.

## 5. Calibration -- is the translated probability honestly better than the market's? (item 4)

**Headline verdict: `TRANSLATION_WORSE_CALIBRATED_THAN_MARKET`.** On the A_primary matched,
held-out universe (n=5,737 player-games, 719 games):

| | Brier | Log-loss |
|---|---|---|
| **Market (de-vigged consensus)** | **0.2488** | **0.6908** |
| Translation (student_t, primary) | 0.2748 | 0.7632 |
| Translation (normal) | 0.2719 | 0.7568 |
| Translation (empirical) | 0.2743 | 0.7594 |
| Translation (het_normal) | 0.2659 | 0.7408 |

Paired, game-date-clustered 95% CI (seed 20260806, 1000 draws, n=254 date clusters), primary
(student_t) minus market: Brier difference **[+0.0211, +0.0312]** -- excludes zero, translation is
worse. Log-loss difference **[+0.0567, +0.0901]** -- same conclusion. The `all_tiers` cell
(n=5,889) agrees: Brier diff **[+0.0234, +0.0343]**.

**This is the honest finding this node's mandate requires reporting plainly, and it is reported
plainly: the model-derived translation does not beat the market's own de-vigged probability on this
universe, regardless of which of the four distributional assumptions is used** (`het_normal` is the
closest, at Brier 0.2659 vs the market's 0.2488, but the gap is still positive and this variant was
never adopted as primary -- see section 3).

**Why, mechanistically (read the reliability tables, not just the summary score).** The market's
de-vigged probabilities are tightly clustered near 0.5 -- 98.9% of A_primary rows fall in the
`[0.4, 0.6)` predicted-probability bin, and within that narrow band the market's observed frequency
tracks its own prediction closely (bin [0.4,0.5): predicted 0.480, observed 0.452; bin [0.5,0.6):
predicted 0.518, observed 0.501). The market is not claiming much confidence, and it is right not
to. The translated model probability spreads much more widely across `[0.05, 0.93]` -- but its
**observed frequency stays flat around 0.45-0.49 in every bin from 0.1 through 0.8** (student_t
variant: bin [0.1,0.2) predicted 0.147, observed 0.526; bin [0.7,0.8) predicted 0.737, observed
0.468). The translation is not miscalibrated in the classic over/under-confident sense at any single
bin -- it is **not discriminating**: claiming 15% or 74% confidence produces almost the same
realized over-rate as claiming 50%. The market's advantage is sharpness earned through restraint,
not through a wider, wrong-shaped confidence claim. Full 10-bin reliability tables for all four
variants and the market, both tiers, are in `FINDINGS.json.calibration.cells`.

## 6. Sensitivity summary (item 2, continued)

| comparison | RMSE (probability) | call-flip rate |
|---|---|---|
| normal vs student_t | 0.011 | 0.0% |
| normal vs empirical | 0.043 | 16.2% |
| normal vs het_normal | 0.030 | 0.0% |

The choice between a parametric homoscedastic distribution (normal or Student-t) barely matters here
-- they agree almost everywhere. The choice to go fully nonparametric (empirical) matters more, and
would have changed the over/under call on roughly one matched player-game in six. Given that finding,
this node's headline calibration verdict (translation loses to market) is **robust to the
parametric-vs-Student-t choice** (both lose, by similar margins) but the exact magnitude of the loss
is sensitive to which of the four variants is read.

## 9. What this node could NOT establish

See `FINDINGS.json.could_not_establish` for the full list (six items), verbatim reasons included.
In summary:

1. A joint MLE of (location, scale, degrees-of-freedom) for the Student-t fit -- no scipy in this
   environment; df was chosen by a log-likelihood grid profile over moment-matched loc/scale, not a
   joint optimizer. The AIC comparison against the normal fit remains valid (both log-likelihoods are
   evaluated the same way on the same data) but the Student-t parameters are not the global MLE.
2. A true parametric skew-normal fit -- not attempted (no scipy); skewness is reported as a moment
   diagnostic only.
3. Calibration conditional on model confidence, injury context, or minutes-uncertainty (e.g. the
   same run's `p_active` output) -- out of this node's scope (translate the point prediction, not
   re-model the player).
4. Any timing, CLV, lead-lag, or stale-window claim from the props archive -- structurally
   prohibited for this T1/T2-descended archive class (D016/P2B; contract section 5) and not
   attempted.
5. Calibration for rebounds/assists/steals/blocks/threes/turnovers -- no matched model-and-market
   data exists for these stat families (section 2).
6. Whether the `het_normal` variant's heteroscedasticity is causally meaningful versus an artifact
   of usage-tier composition -- reported as a fitted sensitivity probe only, never promoted to
   primary regardless of its (very significant) p-value, to avoid tuning the distributional choice
   toward better-looking calibration after the fact.

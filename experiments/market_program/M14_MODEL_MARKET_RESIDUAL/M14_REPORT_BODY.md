## 2. Residual definitions (two scales, four variants, never blended)

Two residual quantities are computed, on purpose kept separate because they carry different
assumptions:

| quantity | formula | assumption it inherits |
|---|---|---|
| `resid_prob` | `p_over_{variant}` (M13 translation) minus `p_over_market_devig` (M11 consensus de-vig) | the model's own out-of-fold residual distribution shape (4 named variants: `normal`, `student_t` [primary], `empirical`, `het_normal`) |
| `resid_points` | `pred_point` (model's raw point estimate) minus `market_implied_mean` (Normal-inversion of the market's de-vigged probability, `MARKET_IMPLIED_PROJECTIONS`) | the market side's **preregistered, unfitted** `sigma=6.0` dispersion constant for `player_points` -- not identified from any price or outcome data in this repository (see section 9) |

`resid_prob_book` / `resid_points_book` are the same two definitions computed against an
**individual book's** own de-vigged probability at the matched consensus line rather than the
cross-book consensus -- this is what makes "by book" reporting possible.

Positive residual = model more bullish on the OVER than the market (either scale). Primary
variant for headline reporting is `student_t` (M13's AIC-selected primary; not re-derived here).

## 5. Acceptance criterion 4 -- what would falsify a claimed model edge

**Hypothesis under test:** does the model-vs-market probability residual (`resid_prob_student_t`)
predict *where the market's own de-vigged probability was wrong* -- i.e. does it covary with
`(y_over - p_over_market_devig)`? This is the `MODEL_VS_MARKET_VALUE` taxonomy class's own
falsification criterion (`TAXONOMY.json`: "shows no predictive content for realized outcomes"),
operationalized here as an OLS slope, cluster-bootstrapped by game date.

**Stated in advance, per this node's mandate (not after seeing the result):**

* **Would be supported by:** slope b > 0, CI excluding zero, pooled **and** stable in sign across
  seasons **and** across all four translation variants **and** not reversed or nulled by
  leave-out-top-N (dropping the most extreme-residual rows).
* **Would be falsified by:** slope CI including zero or negative; sign flipping across seasons;
  appearing under only one translation variant; or collapsing under leave-out-top-N.

**Measured result (A_primary headline, n=5,737):**

| test | slope (b) | 95% CI (clustered, refit-per-draw) | distinguishable from 0? |
|---|---|---|---|
| pooled, primary (student_t) | **-0.0982** | [-0.1813, -0.0097] | **Yes -- negative** |
| by season: 2024 | -0.1447 | [-0.3592, +0.0473] | No |
| by season: 2025 | -0.1353 | [-0.2704, +0.0088] | No |
| by season: 2026 | -0.0327 | [-0.1625, +0.1015] | No |
| by variant: normal | -0.1051 | [-0.1964, -0.0105] | Yes -- negative |
| by variant: empirical | -0.0990 | [-0.1840, -0.0127] | Yes -- negative |
| by variant: het_normal | -0.1111 | [-0.2194, -0.0057] | Yes -- negative |
| drop top 1% \|resid\| | -0.1102 | [-0.2007, -0.0201] | Yes -- negative |
| drop top 5% \|resid\| | -0.1198 | [-0.2349, -0.0133] | Yes -- negative |
| drop top 10% \|resid\| | -0.1059 | [-0.2328, +0.0191] | No |

**Verdict: `FALSIFIED_NO_PREDICTIVE_CONTENT_DETECTED_AT_THIS_N`**, evaluated strictly against the
pooled headline slope, which is the criterion this node committed to in advance. But the honest,
more informative reading goes further than "no signal": the pooled slope is **significantly
negative**, consistently negative and CI-excluding-zero across all four translation variants, and
survives dropping the top 1% and 5% most extreme rows (only losing significance at the top-10%
trim). This is **not noise in the "indistinguishable from zero" sense** -- it is a
directionally consistent, moderately robust finding that **the model's disagreement with the
market is, if anything, a weak signal that the MARKET is right and the model is wrong**, not the
reverse. No individual season reaches significance on its own (power-limited, ~1,700-2,100 rows
per season vs 5,737 pooled), so the negative pooled slope should be read as one robust pooled
estimate rather than three independently-replicated season-level findings.

This is fully consistent with, and mechanistically explains, M13's headline finding (the
translated probability's observed frequency is flat at ~0.45-0.49 across every confidence bin --
it does not discriminate). A translation with no real discriminative content, when it disagrees
more strongly with the market, is not disagreeing *because it knows something the market
doesn't* -- if anything the data here says the opposite direction, weakly. **No model edge is
supported by this analysis. The evidence points toward the model's disagreement with the market
being actively uninformative rather than merely uninformative-neutral.**

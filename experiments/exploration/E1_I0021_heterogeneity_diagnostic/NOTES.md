# E1_I0021 — HETEROGENEITY DIAGNOSTIC — NOTES

Preregistration SHA-256: `8d7c8af4fce21746ce4e1ec3b58dc346a8ea696075b5149c05b9ad4817a96cbb`
Relationships added after preregistration: **0**. Dropped: **0**.

This is a **diagnostic**, not a candidate sweep. It does not look for a new feature. It tests
whether the program's four consecutive nulls on scoring efficiency are artefacts of two specific
construction choices: no realised-minutes floor, and pooling across heterogeneous players.

---

## 1. The two answers

**Does the minutes floor change the picture? No.** Against the strongest prior-only reference facing
the same filtered rows, points-per-minute skill is +0.559% at floor 0, +0.443% at floor 20 and
+0.219% at floor 30. No trend. The user's *mechanism* is nonetheless confirmed: the floor removes
39.3% of points-per-minute variance and 44.7% of its within-player part. The response really was
that noisy. The champion just does not convert the cleaner response into differential skill,
because the naive reference gets the same benefit.

**Is per-player heterogeneity real beyond sampling noise? No — in the coefficients' spread.**
Under the plain within-player shuffle the spread looked 1.09–1.24× too wide (family-wise p = 0.0915
at the preregistered floor; 0.0045–0.0080 at floors 0/10/15). Under a null that *also* preserves
each regressor's serial structure, the excess vanishes at every floor (family-wise p = 0.28–0.66).

**But the coefficients do carry structure along one named axis.** Per-player opponent-defence
sensitivity rises with the player's own strictly-prior usage: Spearman +0.320, family-wise
p = 0.0035 over the six preregistered relationships, both negative controls null. That is a
usage × opponent-defence *interaction* — one extra term in a pooled model, not a per-player model.

## 3. Nulls, and the one that matters

Four schemes were run. All are reported; only one is the verdict.

| Null | What it preserves | What it destroys | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N1 within-player shuffle** | each player's sample size and marginal x | alignment of x to y inside the player, **and x's serial correlation** | the null the directive specifies. **Shown here to be anticonservative for autocorrelated regressors.** |
| **N2 row-level** | nothing player-specific | everything | **contrast only.** Ratios up to 3.61 with p = 0.0005. The inflation this program has confirmed nine times, plainly visible. Never used for a verdict. |
| **N3 team-game block** | that every player on a team shares one opponent value on a night | which opponent a team-game faced | robustness check for the three opponent terms. Ratios 0.96–1.10 — agrees with N1 and N4. |
| **N4 within-player cyclic shift** | each player's marginal x **and its serial correlation** | alignment only | **THE HONEST NULL, and the verdict.** |

### Why N1 and N4 disagree, measured rather than asserted

Across all 48 (floor × relationship) cells,
**corr( lag-1 within-player autocorrelation of x , N1-minus-N4 null ratio gap ) = +0.832.**

| relationship | mean lag-1 acf of x | mean N1−N4 gap |
|---|---|---|
| `NC1_noise` (iid by construction) | −0.029 | −0.004 |
| `NC2_noise` (iid by construction) | −0.025 | −0.004 |
| `R01_prior_efficiency_persistence` | +0.550 | **+0.179** |
| `R06_own_usage` | +0.864 | **+0.121** |

The response is not white inside a player either: its 5-game rolling-mean variance ratio is
1.01–1.04 against 1.00 for white noise, versus 2.6–4.4 for the running-mean regressors. A plain
shuffle destroys the regressor's slow structure while the response keeps its own, so the shuffle
null is too narrow by exactly the overlap between them. **Where x is iid the two nulls agree to
within 0.004; the gap appears only where x is autocorrelated.** That is the mechanism confirmed
on this screen's own data, with its own negative controls as the internal validation.

## 4. `noop_placebo` — and a trap this screen walked up to

Both required controls were run through `screenkit.noop_placebo`. Observed sds are reported, not
rounded.

| Transform | observed sd | distinct draw values | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| identity | 0.000e+00 | 1 | CONFIRMED NO-OP (as designed — the purest control) |
| **relabel the player key and refit per-player coefficients** | **5.207e-17** | 3 | **CONFIRMED NO-OP** |

The second one matters. *"Shuffle the player labels and see whether the coefficient spread
shrinks"* is the control an analyst would most naturally reach for when testing heterogeneity, and
**it tests nothing**: relabelling player ids is a bijection on whole groups, so every player's row
set travels intact to the new label and the multiset of coefficients — and therefore its spread —
is exactly unchanged. This is a new instance of the trap family the program has recorded six times,
in a new place, and it is why the null had to permute x *inside* each player rather than permute
the player key.

### Negative controls

Two preregistered pure-noise columns, one per source frame, run through every path. At the headline
floor: N1 ratios 0.996 and 1.048, N4 ratios 0.997 and 1.043, decisive-structure Spearman p = 0.204
and 0.939. Observed sds are in `pooling_diagnostic.csv` (`n1_null_sd_w`).

One control **did** fire, and it is recorded in §6.

## 5. Screen-kit feedback

**The kit was not modified.** It was imported and used as-is.

### 5a. What worked

- `assert_partition` — clean pass on values across all three frozen frames; no false hit.
- `detect_grouping_level` — correct on all eight regressors. The three opponent terms are constant
  within `team_game`; the three player-history terms have no coarser constant level and the kit
  returns `status = NO_COARSER_LEVEL_EXISTS__ROW_NULL_IS_ANTICONSERVATIVE` with
  `recommended_permutation_level = None`, which is the right refusal.
- `noop_placebo` — caught a genuine no-op at 5.2e-17 and reported the sd rather than rounding it.
  The documented tolerance behaviour is exactly right; a bitwise-zero assertion would have failed.
- `permutation_null(scheme=SCHEME_WITHIN)` — ran without incident at `group_col=["player_id"]`;
  agreed with this screen's vectorised fast path on the real statistic to **5.55e-17**.

### 5b. DEFECT / GAP — the kit has no serial-structure-preserving null

**This is the most important kit finding of this screen, and it is a gap of the same shape as the
row-level-null family the kit already guards against, one level down.**

`SCHEME_WITHIN` shuffles values inside each group. For a regressor that is a **running mean of the
entity's own history** — an extremely common construction in this repository, and the shape of
`refA_*`, `refB_*`, `O01_own_usg_pg`, `P01_c04_prevgame` and every `.shift(1).expanding()` column
the program builds — that shuffle destroys the regressor's autocorrelation and produces a null that
is **too narrow**. Measured here: ratios up to 1.24 at p = 0.0015 on data where the honest null
returns p = 0.39. **A screen that trusted `SCHEME_WITHIN` on such a regressor would have published a
false positive.** This screen nearly did.

The kit's docstring for `detect_grouping_level` already carries the general caveat that it *"does
not prove the recommended level is the right unit of INFERENCE"* — that caveat is what covered us —
but there is no named check and no scheme for it.

**Recommended fix (for the kit's maintainer, not applied here):**
1. Add `SCHEME_WITHIN_CYCLIC` — rotate each group's values by a random offset. It preserves the
   marginal *and* the serial structure and destroys only alignment. ~15 lines; the implementation
   used here is `hd_base.cyclic_shift_within_groups`.
2. Have `permutation_null(scheme=SCHEME_WITHIN)` compute the lag-1 within-group autocorrelation of
   the feature and **warn** above some threshold, naming `SCHEME_WITHIN_CYCLIC`. A silent
   anticonservative default is exactly the failure mode the P2 fix to
   `recommended_permutation_level` was made to prevent.
3. `detect_grouping_level` could report `acf1_within_group` per level as a diagnostic field.

### 5c. Usage nit

`detect_grouping_level(df, feature_col)` raises `KeyError` if the feature is derived later in the
pipeline. Minor; worked around by detecting on the floor-0 twin of the derived column. Not a defect.

## 6. DISCLOSURE — where this screen could have cheated, and one place it did

**Every item below is a real fork where a more flattering number was available.**

1. **Step 1 reference choice.** Scored against the *refit* reference, points-per-minute skill rises
   +0.559% → +4.237%, a 7.6× improvement, and that is a defensible-sounding "we removed the noise
   and the signal appeared". It is not reported as the headline, because at floor 30 the refit
   reference's own MAE is 0.1499 against the frozen reference's 0.1439 — the "gain" is the
   reference getting *worse* from a sample-size penalty. The headline is skill against whichever
   reference is strongest at that floor, which cannot be gamed in either direction. Both columns
   plus the mechanical decomposition are in `minutes_floor_curve.csv`.

2. **Step 2 null choice.** The directive specifies the within-player shuffle. Under it, the
   family-wise test clears at floors 0, 10 and 15 (p = 0.0055, 0.0045, 0.0080) and the headline
   would have been *"per-player heterogeneity is real and pooling has been destroying it"* — the
   most consequential result the program could produce. The cyclic-shift null was added because the
   regressors are autocorrelated, it kills the result everywhere, and it is what is reported.

3. **Floor choice.** Floors 0/10/15 clear the shuffle-null family-wise test; the preregistered
   floor 20 does not. The headline floor was fixed and hashed in `s00_prereg.py` **before any
   statistic was computed**, and the hash is re-verified at the top of every later script.

4. **A CONTROL FIRED AND IT IS REPORTED.** The first pass at step 3 used a precision-weighted
   correlation. Under a covariate-permutation null the pure-noise control NC1 cleared at
   **p = 0.0076** (r = −0.214). The weights are the one player-attached quantity that permutation
   does not permute, so they leak. Every reported structure number was switched to the unweighted
   **rank** statistic, on which the same control is null (p = 0.194). This was caught by the
   preregistered control, not by inspection.

5. **An internal inconsistency is left standing rather than argued away.** The variance share the
   step-3 correlation implies (7–21% for the three opponent terms) exceeds the excess the step-2
   omnibus spread statistic found (~0%). `correlation_robustness.csv` flags all three as
   `reconciles = False`. The rank result stands on its own null and its own controls; the gap is
   recorded as UNRESOLVED in `FINDINGS.json`.

6. **Step 4 could have used leave-one-game-out**, which reads later games and would have improved
   every number. It uses strict expanding walk-forward instead, and every player still comes out
   negative.

7. **2021 was available and would have added 3,114 rows (26%) of power.** It is excluded by default
   so that every figure rests on the same rows as the D081 reproduction. `hd_base` exposes
   `include_2021=True` as a labelled sensitivity; it was not used for any reported number.

8. **The reproduction was run first and could have been skipped.** It is exact to 9.7e-17 across
   all nine of D081's cells (`reproduction_floor0.csv`).

9. **Not done, and disclosed as a limit:** player POSITION and true YEARS OF EXPERIENCE are not
   carried by either frozen frame, so two preregistered step-3 covariates could not be tested.
   `n_prior` is used as an experience proxy and labelled as one. `minutes_tier_prior` had no usable
   source column in the merged frame and is reported as unavailable rather than silently dropped.
